Lead Compensator Circuit

An op-amp lead compensator, hand-built on veroboard, designed to control the altitude of a simulated quadcopter.

What it was

For EEE3094S (Control Systems Engineering), I designed and built an analog circuit to control the altitude of a simulated quadcopter rig ("KittiCopter"). A simple proportional controller from an earlier lab wasn't good enough to meet the settling-time and overshoot targets, so I designed a lead compensator instead, using root-locus analysis to work out where its pole and zero needed to go.

Once I had the compensator's transfer function, I converted it into resistor and capacitor values and hand-wired the circuit on veroboard using OP07 op-amps.

  • Designed the compensator using root-locus analysis, then checked the design in MATLAB/Simulink.
  • Converted the transfer function into resistor and capacitor values and hand-wired the op-amp circuit on veroboard.
  • Added a small filtering resistor after testing picked up noise on the input signal.
  • Bench-tested the circuit against the KittiCopter simulator — it settled faster than required, though overshoot ran a bit higher than target, most likely from resistor tolerances.
