Artefact Spotlight

Designing a HPC cluster on a fixed budget.

The CHPC national round gave every team a budget, a parts list, and five days. My part covered hardware selection, low-level Linux setup, and secure remote access — the layer that let four of us actually run the thing together.

What I owned on the team

Hardware selection under budget, cluster assembly from bare metal, low-level Linux networking (static IPs, NFS shared home directory), and configuring ZeroTier so the whole team had secure SSH access into the cluster from our own laptops — without opening ports on the head node's public firewall.

I also owned the MATLAB Monte Carlo Pi benchmark: installing MATLAB and the Parallel Computing Toolbox across every node via the shared NFS directory, then tuning parfor loops and parpool configurations (threads vs. processes vs. local) to get the best throughput for our core count.

Reflection

This project drew directly on coursework in networking, computer architecture, and parallel computing — but the bigger lesson was leadership. As team captain, I learned to delegate and check in across four workstreams at once so every benchmark kept moving under a five-day clock. It's the closest I've come to the real trade-offs of taking a design from a budget spreadsheet through to a working, benchmarked system.
