Four weeks inside a working consultancy.

Vacation work internship at Henry Fagan, in their electrical engineering department — gaining exposure to the software and site-verification processes used in professional electrical services design.

What I worked on

My work centred on two tools used across the electrical consulting industry: AutoCAD, for creating and amending electrical layout drawings, and Relux, a lighting-simulation package that checks a proposed luminaire layout against the illuminance (lux) level required for a space. Every design had to comply with SANS 10142-1 — which meant reading the standard for the minimum lux level per room type, and working through wiring-size calculations by hand.

  • Ran Relux simulations for each room type — matching light fittings to the required lux level and adjusting luminaire layouts until the simulation met SANS 10142-1.
  • Calculated appropriate cable sizes for different purposes (lighting, alarm, wifi circuits) per SANS guidelines, and reviewed existing wiring layouts for deviations in cable sizing and circuit protection.
  • Updated live electrical services projects in AutoCAD — cable routing, fixture layouts, and alarm systems — to reflect design changes from the engineers on the team.
  • Carried out on-site checks, visiting project sites to confirm installed electrical work matched the approved drawings.
  • Edited Word and Excel project documentation, and called light suppliers for pricing and availability.
  • Closed out the internship with a PowerPoint presentation to the whole office on what I'd done and learned.

Results

By the end of the internship I could run a Relux simulation from start to finish and construct and edit AutoCAD drawings independently. Reviewing wiring layouts against SANS standards helped me spot design issues that needed correcting, and the site checks taught me how to verify a build actually matches what was drawn.

Reflection

This internship gave me real exposure to the consulting engineering profession — beyond the technical work, sitting in on client meetings and staff meetings showed me how multi-disciplinary teams actually deliver a project. It also gave SANS a context I hadn't had from coursework alone: using it on a live project rather than treating it as an arbitrary set of numbers. Seeing my own lighting layouts actually installed on-site was the most rewarding part, and it's what made me excited to one day run a project of my own.
